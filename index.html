04Big data · Analytics

Aramark Procurement Analytics

Maps supplier concentration risk across Aramark’s 2025 US procurement spend — 43M rows in BigQuery. No state has a competitive distributor market, weather measurably shifts what gets bought, and k-means finds six buying archetypes.

FindingOne distributor controls 54% of US spend — antitrust risk quantified with the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index

05Data visualization

AWS EC2 Pricing Landscape

Treats the whole AWS EC2 price list as a dataset — 318,604 prices, 1,337 instance types, 104 regions — to ask how AWS really prices compute. Pareto testing finds the 6.2% on the efficiency frontier, and PCA projects the catalogue into two dimensions.

FindingThe saving is Graviton, not a newer generation — 15–20% cheaper than Intel at matched generation and size

Built withPython · pandas · NumPy · scikit-learn · PCA · Plotly · Jupyter

About

I pick the problem first and learn whatever it takes to build it. For my research that meant writing a deep-learning architecture in PyTorch from nothing, with no prior PyTorch, and pushing accuracy from 54.7% to 82.1%. At Ramedia it meant shipping two production platforms end to end, wiring up payments, real-time sync and a handful of third-party APIs as the work called for them.

The projects I care about most started the same way. A few months ago I went to an assistive-technology workshop and got talking to the speaker afterwards. She told me that when a blind person loses something at home, their only real option is to video-call a stranger and ask them to look through the camera. I could not stop thinking about it. BlindSpot came out of that conversation — the award came later, and it was never the point.

That is the pattern: I build things nobody asked for, and the findings I am proudest of came from questions nobody assigned me. Right now I am finishing my MS in Computer Science at Rutgers and looking for somewhere to keep doing it.

I’ve never picked a project because I already knew the stack.

A bit better each time.

Away from the screen I train, I read, and I spend a probably unreasonable amount of attention on habits — the small things you repeat until they compound into something. It is the same instinct as the software: nothing good arrives finished, you just keep making it slightly better than it was.
